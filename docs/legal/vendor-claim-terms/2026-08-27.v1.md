## Read this first

Plain English is a requirement here, not a style choice. Consent under the **Nigeria Data Protection
Act 2023** must be *informed*, and text a shopkeeper cannot read is not informed consent. Every
sentence below is meant to be understood by the person actually signing up — someone running a shop,
on a phone, in the middle of a working day.

**Every statement here matches what the software actually does.** Where the software does something
awkward — like knowing about your account before you ever heard of us — this says so plainly rather
than leaving it out.

---

## 1. Who we are

Amana operates a wallet that lets people control how money they give to others is spent. We are the
**data controller** for the information described here.

Contact for anything in this notice, including all the rights in section 7: **[SUPPORT CHANNEL — TO
BE FILLED IN BEFORE LAUNCH]**.

## 2. How we already had a record of your account

This is the part most people are surprised by, so it comes first.

**You did not sign up to be listed. We built a record of your bank account because Amana users paid
you.** When someone using Amana sends money to your account, our system keeps a note of:

- your bank and account number
- the account name your bank returns
- what the payer said they were buying
- which household paid you — **as an internal reference, never their identity shown to you**

Once **at least five different households** have paid the same account, we create a merchant record
for it. Nobody is contacted at that point, and nothing about you is shown publicly. The record only
exists so that when an Amana user pays you again, we can tell them who they are paying and apply
their own spending rules correctly.

**Claiming your account is how you take control of that record.** You may also ask us to remove it —
see section 7.

## 3. What claiming does

To claim, you prove two things: that you hold the phone number you gave us, and that your bank
record links that phone to that account. We check the second with NIBSS through our banking partner.

Once claimed:

- Your **bank-verified business name** replaces whatever payers had typed. Nobody can put words on
  your listing but you and us.
- You get an **Amana Vendor Code** (like `AMNV-7QK2H-9PZ0R`) and a public page for it.
- You choose your **spending category**, which decides whose spending rules allow paying you.

**Your public page shows:** your business name, that you are verified on Amana, and **the last four
digits of your account number only**. It does not show your phone number, your full account number,
who has paid you, or how much.

## 4. What we keep, and for how long

| What | Why | How long |
|---|---|---|
| Bank and account number, business name | To identify you as a payee | While your record exists |
| Your phone number | To prove the claim was yours, and to reach you | While your record exists |
| Payments to you, recorded one household at a time | To recognise you and keep your category accurate | **180 days**, then deleted automatically |
| Your consent choices | To prove what you agreed to, and when | While your record exists |

Individual payment records are deleted after **180 days**. Your merchant record itself lasts until
you ask us to remove it.

## 5. The two things you are agreeing to — and they are separate

**A. These terms (required).** You cannot claim an account without accepting this notice. It is what
allows us to hold your record, show your page, and let people pay you.

**B. Being introduced to a lender (optional, and off unless you say yes).**

Because we can see how regularly different customers pay you, we can vouch for a business that banks
and lenders would otherwise have no way to assess. Many small businesses in Nigeria are refused
credit simply because nobody can see their trade. If you say yes, we may introduce you to a lender
or credit partner.

**Saying no costs you nothing.** Your claim goes through exactly the same way, your page works the
same way, and we will not ask again unless you raise it. **You can change your mind at any time**
(section 7).

We record your answer either way — including a no — so that we have proof we asked and proof we
respected the answer.

## 6. What we will never do

- **We will not sell your information.**
- **We will not introduce you to anyone unless you have said yes**, in the specific way described in
  5B.
- **We will not share anything about the people who pay you.** What they buy and what they spend is
  theirs, not yours and not ours to trade.
- **We will not put anything on your public page that you did not give us or that your bank did not
  confirm.**

## 7. Your rights

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 you may, at any time:

- **See** everything we hold about you
- **Correct** anything wrong
- **Withdraw** your consent to the lender introduction, without giving a reason and without losing
  anything else
- **Ask us to delete** your merchant record entirely
- **Complain** to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission if you think we have handled your
  information badly

**How to do any of these today: contact support** at the channel in section 1, and we will action it
and record that we did.

> **Being honest about a limitation.** The law says withdrawing consent should be as easy as giving
> it, and right now it is not — you gave it by tapping a box, and you withdraw it by contacting us.
> We are building a way to do it yourself. Until that exists, contacting support is the way, and our
> team is instructed to action it without argument.

## 8. If we suspend your listing

We may suspend a merchant record — for example if we believe an account is being misused. A
suspended listing stops being enforceable for spending rules, and stops being publicly shown. We
keep the record so that the decision is auditable, and you can contact us to dispute it.

## 9. Changing this text

This notice carries a version, and your agreement is recorded against **the version you were
actually shown**. If we change what this says in any way that matters, we publish a new version and
ask you again. **Agreeing to v1 is not agreeing to v2.**

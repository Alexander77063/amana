# Consent, privacy and what must never be on screen

Filming real people spending real money in a real market. Two separate obligations, easy to
conflate:

1. **Consent to be filmed** — the people, the vendors, the market.
2. **Data protection** — what appears on the phone screens, which is financial data about real
   people and is governed by the **NDPR** (Nigeria Data Protection Regulation) and the NDPA 2023.

The second one is the one teams get wrong, because a screen recording of a working app is a
recording of somebody's account details.

> Not legal advice. Have a Nigerian lawyer review the release form and the NDPR position before
> the shoot. What follows is the practical shape and the specific Amana risks.

---

## Never on screen, in any frame, in any cut

Check this list **in the edit, frame by frame**, not just on the day:

| Must not appear | Where it will catch you out |
| --- | --- |
| **BVN or NIN** | `VerifyScreen` during signup — Chapter 1 of the onboarding cut films exactly this screen |
| **Full bank account numbers** | `AccountEntryScreen`, `ConfirmScreen`, the vendor's own bank alert, and the top-up account on `HomeDashboardScreen` |
| **Phone numbers** | `PhoneScreen`, `PhoneLookupScreen`, pairing screens, and the vendor's phone when it buzzes |
| **Real balances of a real household** | `HomeDashboardScreen` |
| **A vendor's earnings or history** | Their bank alert may show a running balance |
| **Anyone's face who has not signed** | Market crowds move; a wide shot catches dozens |

**How to satisfy this without ruining the shot:** use **purpose-made accounts and numbers created
for the video**, on phones whose SIMs were bought for it, with a master wallet funded with a known
small amount. Then nothing on screen belongs to a person's real financial life, and a frame that
slips through is embarrassing rather than harmful.

Where a real vendor's account is genuinely used — and it should be, that is the point — frame so
the number is not legible, or blur it in post. **Blur in post is a fallback, not a plan**: it fails
when someone forgets, and the raw footage still exists.

## Consent — who signs what

### Principal and agent (the cast)

Full written release: filming, name and likeness, use in investor materials, on the website, in
app-store listings and in social media, with no time limit and no fee beyond what was agreed.
If either is **under 18**, a parent or guardian signs, and the child assents too.

### Vendors

This is the group most likely to be treated casually and most deserving of care. A trader agreeing
in the middle of a busy morning has not really agreed.

- Approach **before the shoot day**, not during.
- Explain plainly: what the video is for, that investors will see it, that it may go online, that
  their stall and their face may appear.
- Written release, in a language they read comfortably — have **Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa/Pidgin**
  translations ready as appropriate to the market.
- Pay for their time. It is their working day. Agree the amount up front, in writing, and make it
  clear it is for time and not for saying anything in particular.
- **A vendor may decline and still take the payment.** Their business is not conditional on being
  filmed, and it should be said out loud.

### Bystanders and the crowd

- **Signage at the entrance to the filming area** stating that filming is in progress, with a
  contact, in English and the local language.
- Anyone in focus and identifiable needs a release. Incidental background crowd in a public market
  is normally acceptable — confirm with the lawyer.
- **If someone objects, stop filming them and delete the footage in front of them.** No argument.
  This costs a shot; the alternative costs the market's goodwill, and you have to go back.

### The market itself

Most Lagos markets have a **traders' association or market authority**. Get permission from them
before the day, in writing if possible. Turning up with a camera unannounced is how a shoot gets
shut down mid-morning, and a crew standing idle is the most expensive thing on the schedule.

## NDPR, concretely

- You are processing personal and financial data of identifiable people for a **new purpose**
  (marketing), not the purpose they gave it for (using a wallet). That needs its **own consent**,
  separate from the app's terms.
- The release form should state what is collected, what it is used for, how long it is kept, and
  that consent can be withdrawn.
- **Withdrawal has to mean something.** Decide now what you will do if a vendor asks in six months
  to be removed from a video that is on the website and in a deck. The honest answer — that
  distributed copies cannot all be recalled — belongs on the form, in plain words.
- Keep signed releases somewhere durable and access-controlled, not in a camera bag. Keep them as
  long as the footage is in use, and no longer.

---

## The release form — what it must contain

Have a lawyer draft it. It needs, at minimum:

1. Name, signature, date, and a phone number for follow-up.
2. What is being filmed and where.
3. Where it may be used: investor presentations, website, app stores, social media, press.
4. That use may be **indefinite and worldwide**.
5. That no further payment is owed.
6. What data was captured (image, voice, stall name, and — if shown — transaction details).
7. How to withdraw consent, and the honest limits of withdrawal.
8. A guardian's signature block for anyone under 18.

Bring **more copies than you think**, on paper. Phone signatures fail in sunlight and flat
batteries.

---

## After the shoot

- **Review every frame** against the list at the top before anything is shared, including internal
  decks. A deck leaks.
- Store raw footage with access control. It contains more than the cut does.
- Keep a simple register: who signed, what they consented to, where the form is.
- If a payment is shown, keep a note of which transaction it was — if anyone ever asks whether it
  was real, you want to be able to show that it was.

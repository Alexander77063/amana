# Logistics and casting

## Choosing the market

What matters, in order:

1. **Vendors who already take bank transfers.** This is the whole premise — the money lands in the
   trader's account and they needed no onboarding. In a market where everyone insists on cash, the
   central shot does not exist. Ask before committing.
2. **Someone who can introduce you.** A trader who knows the association, or a regular customer
   with standing. Cold-approaching stalls with a camera produces suspicion and bad footage.
3. **Light and space.** You need room to stand back for a wide, and somewhere shaded to shoot
   phone screens. Direct midday sun on a phone screen is unusable footage.
4. **Noise you can live with.** Market sound is an asset for atmosphere and a liability for
   dialogue. Plan to record voiceover separately; do not rely on capturing usable speech.
5. **Somewhere you can return to.** You will miss something. A market where you are welcome twice
   is worth more than a more photogenic one where you are not.

## Vendors

- Brief **four to six** for a script needing two or three. People close stalls, travel, forget.
- Visit **a few days before**. Explain the video, agree payment for their time, get the release
  signed then — not in the rush of the morning.
- **Confirm each bank account with a real ₦100 test transfer in advance.** A wrong account number
  on the day sends real money to a stranger while a camera watches.
- Prefer vendors selling things that **look good and read instantly**: food, produce, fabric.
  Something the viewer recognises without a caption.
- Tell them plainly they can change their mind, and that the payment for their goods stands either
  way.

## Cast

| Role | Who | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Principal** | A parent or a small-business owner, ideally an actual target user | Must be filmed **genuinely elsewhere** for the approval scene — office, home, a different part of the market. Not standing off-camera. |
| **Agent** | Their teenager, or a member of their staff | The person a viewer imagines being sent to the market. If under 18, guardian consent (see the consent doc). |
| **Vendors** | Real traders, as themselves | Not actors. It shows. |

**Use real users if you possibly can.** A genuine user handles the app differently from someone
performing — the hesitations are real, and an investor reads that as truth. A rehearsed actor
produces glossy footage that says nothing about whether the product works.

## Devices, SIMs and connectivity

- **Two phones minimum** (principal and agent), **plus one spare of each**. Native EAS builds
  installed and signed in **days before**, not on the morning.
- **NFC on at least one pair** — the tap-to-pair shot is the best thirty seconds of the onboarding
  cut and needs hardware support.
- **SIMs bought for the video**, on the network with the best coverage in that specific market —
  test it there, not at the office. Coverage inside a covered market is frequently bad.
- **Data plans with real headroom.** Push notification delivery is the pinch point of the bump
  scene.
- Power banks per phone. Screen-max brightness plus camera plus data drains a battery in a couple
  of hours.
- **Silence every other app.** One WhatsApp banner across a receipt is a reshoot.

## Schedule — half a day, realistically

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Day before** | Full rehearsal with the real phones and SIMs, in the market if allowed. Time the bump round trip. Confirm vendors. Charge everything. |
| **07:00** | Crew arrives, market is waking. Best light, fewest crowds. Shoot the wides and the B-roll now. |
| **08:00** | Onboarding cut: setup, pairing, rules. Quiet, controlled, in shade. |
| **09:30** | The market is busy. Shoot the real payment and the vendor's alert. **The priority shot.** |
| **10:30** | The bump: agent over-limit, parent approving from elsewhere. Allow more time than you think. |
| **11:15** | The refusal, cutaways, anything missed. |
| **12:00** | Wrap. Pay vendors. Thank the association. |

**Shoot the most important thing before you are tired and before the market peaks.** If the day
collapses, the payment and the vendor's alert are what you cannot go home without.

## Kit

- Camera capable of a clean wide **and** a tight phone-screen shot. A second body saves a lot of
  lens-swapping in a crowd.
- **Lavalier or shotgun mic** if anyone speaks on camera. Market noise makes on-camera dialogue
  unusable otherwise.
- Small **reflector or diffuser** for phone screens in daylight — the single most useful cheap item.
- Gimbal for the walking shots, if there is room. Many markets do not have room.
- **Printed release forms**, more than you need, plus pens.
- Cash for vendor payments — and note that paying vendors in cash, for a video about not using
  cash, is fine and unremarkable; do not try to make a point of it.

## Budget shape

Not a quote, just the line items people forget: crew day rate, vendor time payments, market
association fee or courtesy, SIMs and data, transport for cast and crew, food for a half-day,
contingency for a second visit. **Assume you will need the second visit.**

## After the day

- Back up footage **twice, before leaving the market** if you can, or immediately after. There is
  no reshoot of a real moment.
- Review against the never-film list in `consent-and-privacy.md` before anything goes in a deck.
- Note which transactions were filmed, so their reality can be demonstrated later if anyone asks.
